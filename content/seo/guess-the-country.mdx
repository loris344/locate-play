Country-level guessing is the more achievable version of this format, and it's worth treating separately from city-level guessing rather than as a lesser version of the same task — the clue set that gets you to the right country is genuinely different from what it takes to name the exact city.

## Why guess the country from an adult scene attracts location-game players

A country-level guess only needs one or two solid, corroborating clues rather than the dense, specific detail a city guess requires. That makes it the more consistently reachable target in almost every round, and the right place to build the underlying skill before attempting city-level precision.

## Photo, video, Street View and scene-based formats

The clue types that carry the most weight for country identification transfer well regardless of source format: outlet and switch shape, window and shutter construction, any visible text or language, and — when the audio is usable — accent or spoken language in the background. A Street View round gives you road signage and driving side on top of that; an interior video clip usually doesn't, which is what makes the country-level guess in this format slightly harder than its Street View equivalent despite needing less precision than a city guess.

## How map scoring and clue reading change by format

Because scoring rewards proximity rather than exact matches, a confident, well-reasoned country guess is close to the score ceiling even without a specific city attached — the marginal points from correctly guessing the exact city on top of the right country are smaller than most players expect. That makes country-level accuracy the highest-value skill to build first.
