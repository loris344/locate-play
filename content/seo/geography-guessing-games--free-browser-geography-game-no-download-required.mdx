"No download required" is a real technical distinction, not just marketing language. A genuine browser game runs entirely in HTML5/JavaScript with no native install step; a lot of things marketed as "free browser games" still funnel you toward a store listing or a plugin partway through.

## Why free browser game attracts location-game players

The appeal is speed to first round: click a link, you're playing within seconds, no app store detour and nothing left behind afterward. For a genre built around short, replayable rounds, that friction-free entry matters more than it does for a longer-session game genre.

## Photo, video, Street View and scene-based formats

Browser-native implementations exist across every format in this genre, but they're not equally lightweight. A full interactive Street View panorama is the heaviest to load and run in-browser; single photos and short video clips are considerably lighter, since there's no panoramic streaming involved — just one image or a short file to load per round.

## How map scoring and clue reading change by format

Load performance aside, scoring works the same everywhere: distance from your pin to the real location determines your points. What genuinely differs by format is pacing — a lightweight photo or clip format can move from round to round almost instantly, while a full panorama needs a moment to load and initialize each time, which changes how a session actually feels to play even when the underlying scoring math is identical.
