Guessing a specific city is a different, harder task than guessing a country, and it's worth treating it that way. Country-level clues (language, driving side, general climate) get you into the right region; city-level identification needs something denser and more specific than that.

## Why guess the city from an adult scene attracts location-game players

City-level guessing is the format's highest-difficulty, highest-reward mode. Getting a country right from an interior scene is already a real skill; naming the actual city on top of that means the scene had to hand you something unusually specific — a skyline sliver through a window, a transit map or sign, or an architectural style tied tightly to one city rather than a whole country.

## Photo, video, Street View and scene-based formats

In a Street View game, city-level guesses lean heavily on things you can look around for: road markings, building density, signage language. In a scene-based format working from a single interior clip, most of that is gone — city identification instead comes down to whatever background detail happens to be dense enough to be city-specific rather than merely country-specific. That's rarer, which is exactly why nailing a city (rather than just a country) feels disproportionately satisfying when it happens.

## How map scoring and clue reading change by format

Distance-based scoring rewards being close even when you can't commit to an exact city — a guess placed confidently in the right metro area still scores well even if you got the district wrong. The practical strategy that follows from this: don't force a specific city guess when the scene has only given you country-level evidence. A well-placed regional guess consistently beats an overconfident, wrong city pin.
